About

Security work, from three different angles

I'm a junior at Georgia Tech studying Computer Engineering with a formal concentration in Cybersecurity and Distributed Systems. Over the past two years I've worked blue team, threat intelligence, and offensive testing — which has been the fastest way I've found to understand how the pieces actually fit together.

Being Brazilian has turned out to be a genuine advantage in this field. A large part of the cybercrime ecosystem I researched at KELA — PIX fraud kits, stealer logs, carding operations — runs in Portuguese, on platforms most analysts can't read. That's a niche I intend to keep building on.

Before security, I swam. Twelve years of competitive swimming, four of them as a club captain in Brazil, and a run as a top-10 all-time performer in Georgia Tech program history and a multiple-time Brazilian junior national champion. Competing at that level while carrying a full engineering load taught me the thing this work rewards most: showing up consistently for something that only pays off much later.

I like learning by building and breaking things — understanding how systems fail, how they're misused, and how to design them so they hold.
