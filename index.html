01

Voice Deepfake Detection for Brazilian Portuguese

In progress.

Problem

Detect synthetic speech in Brazilian Portuguese, including audio that has passed through a phone line.

What I built

A probe on WavLM features that labels clips as real or synthetic, with a zero persistence design for LGPD: audio is processed and not stored.

2.6%Equal error rate for the WavLM probe
+10.3Points of error added by G.711 phone audio on unseen generators
4Dataset shortcuts found that the model could exploit
2,481Clips misaligned by a bug I found

What went wrong

Auditing the data turned up four shortcuts the model could learn instead of the voice itself, and a bug that misaligned 2,481 clips.

What I learned

Phone audio is the hard case. Passing audio through G.711 raised the error by 10.3 points on generators the model had not seen.

02

Malware Analysis Platform

In progress.

Problem

Triage Windows PE files offline, so nothing leaves the host.

What I built

A self-hosted service: upload a sample, parse it with pefile, match it against YARA rules, and persist the results.

It runs as a three-service Docker Compose stack (database, API, frontend), with named volumes for sample storage and rules mounted read-only.

Where it stands

Classification is a heuristic placeholder today. Supervised ML and MITRE ATT&CK technique mapping are the next milestones.

How it is tested

Tested with pytest and httpx, type checked with mypy, and formatted with ruff and black.

03

World Cup Prediction Model

Problem

Predict the 48-team World Cup from international match results, then check the predictions against what actually happened.

What I built

A model trained on 49,000+ men's international match results since 1872, weighting recent matches more heavily so it reads current form over history.

I calibrated the randomness until favorites looked beatable rather than certain, then ran 20,000 simulations of the full tournament.

61%Results called correctly through the semifinals, 62 of 102
73%Correct in the knockout rounds
11Exact scorelines
20,000Simulations of the full tournament

What went wrong

Against betting markets, the largest disagreement was Argentina: 15% in my model versus roughly 8%. I traced it to a real blind spot. The model sees only results, not squad age, injuries, or club form. That is what I am adding next.

What I learned

I tested a simple Elo rating system head to head against a Dixon-Coles goal model on the last four World Cups. Elo predicted champions better, so I shipped it and dropped the more complex model.

Stack
Python, pandas, NumPy, SciPy, Elo ratings, Dixon-Coles, Monte Carlo simulation
